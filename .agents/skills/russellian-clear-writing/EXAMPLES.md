# Russellian Clear Writing — Examples

Three before/after rewrites, one per practical test from Russell's "How I Write" (1956).

## Test 1: Prefer the shorter exact word

Before:

> The implementation of the proposed methodological framework enables the optimization of communicative clarity in academic prose.

After:

> The method helps academic prose become clearer.

Reason:

The second sentence says the same thing with fewer and plainer words.

## Test 2: Separate qualifications instead of overloading

Before:

> The model is useful when the data are complete, the labels are reliable, the sampling process is stable, and the goal is exploratory rather than predictive, although these conditions are rarely all satisfied.

After:

> The model is useful under specific conditions. The data must be complete, the labels reliable, and the sampling process stable. It is better suited to exploration than prediction. In practice, these conditions are not always satisfied.

Reason:

The qualifications are easier to judge when separated.

## Test 3: Do not betray the reader's expectation

Before:

> The data, collected over five years and analyzed by three independent teams using rigorous methods, turned out to be unusable.

After:

> The data were collected over five years and analyzed by three independent teams. The methods were rigorous. Despite this, the data turned out to be unusable.

Reason:

The revision does not lead the reader toward confidence and then abruptly reverse direction. It separates the grounds for confidence from the final qualification.
